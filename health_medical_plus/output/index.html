New approach could make curative lung cancer treatment possible for more patients

Despite significant advances in treatment, lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide. A clinical trial recently published in Nature Medicine suggests that a more flexible approach to treating locally advanced non-small-cell lung cancer could open up new treatment options for patients. By starting with a combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy (durvalumab), followed by reassessment by a multidisciplinary team, doctors may be able to offer curative surgery to more patients. The study also suggests that patients who remain ineligible for surgery after this initial treatment may still have another potentially curative option: a combination of chemotherapy and radiation therapy.
